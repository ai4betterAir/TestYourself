CONCEPT 1

What is a fraction?

A fraction describes equal parts of a whole. The bottom number is the denominator: it tells us how many equal parts make the whole. The top number is the numerator: it tells us how many of those parts we have.

3
5

3 = numerator
We have 3 parts.

5 = denominator
The whole has 5 equal parts.

Remember: the parts must be equal. If a shape is cut into pieces of different sizes, those pieces do not correctly represent one fraction of the whole.
CONCEPT 2

Equivalent fractions

Equivalent fractions look different but have the same value. If we multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number, the value stays the same.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Find a fraction equivalent to 3/5 with denominator 20.

5 × 4 = 20, so multiply the numerator by 4 too: 3 × 4 = 12.

Therefore 3/5 = 12/20 ✓
CONCEPT 3

Comparing fractions

If fractions have the same denominator, compare their numerators. If the denominators are different, first make equivalent fractions with a common denominator.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Which is larger: 2/3 or 3/4?

Use denominator 12:   2/3 = 8/12   and   3/4 = 9/12.

9/12 is greater than 8/12, so 3/4 > 2/3 ✓
CONCEPT 4

Fractions on a number line

Fractions are numbers, so they have exact positions on a number line. Divide the distance from 0 to 1 into equal parts according to the denominator.
