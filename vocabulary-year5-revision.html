FULL ADVENTURE STORY

The Sky Star Quest

Ten-year-old Ari was usually cheerful, but that morning he felt a little drowsy as he opened his science notebook. His teacher said the class would hold an annual invention fair, and Ari generally loved projects, but today one variable could change everything: the mayor might veto the fair unless Ari built something outstanding. He ran to purchase a small crystal battery, but the shopkeeper warned him that the path beyond town was hazardous. A novice hero might make a blunder, especially if he acted rash. Ari put the battery in a metal receptacle, felt his hands quiver, and looked across the vast hills. Somewhere out there, a secret foe had hidden the Sky Star, and Ari knew he had to reply to the challenge.

The first forest was brutal and treacherous. Ari felt bewildered, but remembered that courage could be bountiful even when strength was small. A glowing bird gave him a valid clue: wake the dormant gate before its ceaseless bells rang. The task looked daunting and the gate was cumbersome, but beyond it was a blissful valley. At the valley entrance, a mirror judge offered Ari criticism but also one compliment: he had a unique heart. The bridge looked flimsy, so the judge would not allow anyone to cross without vigor. Ari felt fatigue, yet used a substantial vine to steady himself. The rules would prohibit shortcuts.

A frisky dragon cub showed Ari a permanent trail. It was tiresome, and a sluggish robot guard gave him a ridiculous riddle. Ari stayed considerate and answered sensibly, even when the ground became unstable because the guard was heedless with a lever. Then an earthquake shook a purple vineyard and a glowing whirlpool opened near a silver tower. Ari found the rebels’ headquarters, a guidebook, blueprint and cracked windshield. A masterpiece on the wall left him spellbound until a message flashed: score a courage touchdown before sunset.

A music fairy gave Ari a lute and invited him on a moonlit cruise. The river was not foul, but the course was difficult because pirate crews had stolen the fairy’s loot. A white fowl flew past while a horse with a golden bridle carried a bridal banner. The rocks were coarse, but music showed the safe way. At the next gate, Ari had to present a token and prove he was present in mind and heart. A minute crystal appeared for one minute. A guard tried to refuse him entry and kicked aside refuse. An invalid traveller needed help while a sign said Ari’s password was invalid. Ari lifted an object shaped like a star and did not object when asked to share it.

The gate opened into a festival with sardines, tuxedos, vaudeville music, a bikini, a mini marathon, cologne, bologna, a tarantula, tangerine and cantaloupe. Past it, an alligator wearing pajamas guarded a bridge. A bandit poured syrup on the stones and tried to sell Ari a fake magazine. A wise sheik in a kimono offered okra at a barbecue while an impala pointed toward the desert library. A coed arrived in a taxi with a ref, limo and champ. A grad tested a mike as every fan cheered. Ari found a curio, made the engine rev and raced toward the mountains.

Paint began to splatter across the sky, drawing a squiggle map. A metal bird gave a squawk as paratroops floated through a flurry. A glimmer led Ari to a medevac ship beside a spacelab. A telethon played on screen and a flare marked the next clue. Ari reached a living island where a colony of bees, knot of toads and skulk of foxes met. A company of actors carried a string of lanterns, a gaggle of geese flew over a school of fish, and a flower bed stood near a gang of rabbits and troop of scouts.

The map showed an isthmus, peninsula, strait, delta, oasis, tributary, valley, gorge, plateau and archipelago. On a floating island a poet asked Ari for rhyme and meter, a simile, couplet and personification. Ari added a haiku, metaphor, alliteration, onomatopoeia and sonnet. A funny wizard carried a doodad and hodgepodge bag and loved chitchat. He called fear namby-pamby, shouted fiddlesticks, tried to flabbergast Ari, warned him not to lollygag, caused a hullabaloo, and sent a rapscallion monkey running with the map. Ari focused on the nitty-gritty.

Ari chased it to a pedestal shaped like a pedal. Each numeral made him enumerate a brave choice. A liberal king allowed every pedestrian to pass, including a biped alien with numerous hats. Ari named the numerator and the gate of liberty opened. He needed clarity, asked a crystal to clarify the path and heard a clarion bell. A book tried to predict the future with strange diction. Ari had to declare a promise, read a declaration, resist anyone trying to dictate his choices, and pass a tiny dictator beetle guarding a dictionary.

At a workshop, a mechanic fixed a moon bike. Its wheels had a perfect diameter; a thermometer watched the heat and a speedometer measured star-speed. Ari felt sympathy for the mechanic and helped mechanize a pulley. A barometer warned of storms, a kilometer marker glowed, and a pathetic robot studied pathology. Ari then used scuba gear in a crystal lake. Radar and sonar found a lost modem near a quasar shell. He ate food cooked in canola oil, used a zip line and laser, and everything became a snafu when a tiny veep crab tried to become captain.

A tunnel led to a city with a pram, larder, flat, underground, chemist, lift, cupboard, holiday, nappy and cutlery. At the palace Ari found an album and ketchup shaped like a comet. A dancer in a leotard warned of a cyclone. A dahlia glowed with magical oxygen from a manuscript. The king’s academy trained zany warriors to stop a volcano.

The volcano spell was retroactive. An irresponsible goblin caused a malfunction at an intersection and tried to abduct the Sky Star. Ari took a retrospective look at the clues. The plan was irrational and malformed, so he chose not to interpose anger and decided to abstain from fighting until he understood. A bivalve shell could commiserate with anyone lonely. It warned that stealing was illegal, but the thief escaped in a hydroplane singing in a monotone. A biannual race began, so Ari had to compile clues. An illiterate ogre could not read the hydroelectric sign, but knew one monosyllable: help.

At the thunder arena, a dentist dragon checked a warrior’s teeth. The crowd called Ari heroic, but the thief made an accusation that Ari wanted fame. Ari answered with optimism while turbulent clouds shook the roof. A perfectionist bird arranged every feather, a historic bell rang, and everyone chose recreation instead of war. A reporter from dragon journalism wrote that the succulent fruit feast tasted better than victory. Ari returned the Sky Star, and every word he had learned shone like a tiny power inside him.
