# Australian drafting punctuation (reference)

Staff note for SkillUP. Not Year 6 lesson text. Sources: Commonwealth Office of Parliamentary Counsel (OPC) Drafting Direction 2.1 and Plain English Manual; NSW PCO plain language policy (DP2).

## Semicolons in legislation

- A paragraph that is **not** the end of a section or subsection ends with a **semicolon**.
- The last paragraph of the section takes a **full stop**.
- Put **and** or **or** only on the final item. That word chooses cumulative vs alternative logic.
- Every semicolon item must still sit under the same lead-in verb (*The Minister may:* …).

Example shape:

    The Minister may:
    (a) grant the licence; and
    (b) impose a condition.

## Commas

- Punctuation is part of the legal text.
- Do not add a comma only for rhythm. If it does no legal work, recast the sentence.
- Do not join two full sentences with a comma alone (comma splice).

## Plain language (OPC / NSW PCO)

- Short sentences. Subject next to verb.
- Active voice. Avoid double negatives.
- Lists in paragraphs, not one 70-word line.
- Plain = clear and precise, not simplistic.
- Spelling: Macquarie Dictionary.

## School vs statute

| School writing | Legislative drafting |
|---|---|
| One semicolon between two related sentences | Semicolon = this item is still inside the same sentence of law |
| Prefer a full stop if the reader needs a breath | Prefer a new subsection if the item is a separate duty |
